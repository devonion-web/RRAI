Refactor the RFP / RFI capability.

Current issue:

The capability is focused on:
- extracting requirements
- classifying ownership
- drafting responses

This is the wrong primary objective.

Risk Rising does not begin an RFP by writing responses.

Risk Rising begins by understanding:

- what the customer is trying to achieve
- what business outcomes are being sought
- what use cases exist
- what capabilities are required
- what modules are in scope
- what implementation effort is implied
- what integrations are required
- what support obligations exist
- what commercial implications exist
- what RR needs to respond to

The capability should behave like a senior RR consultant reviewing an opportunity for the first time.

NEW PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Rename the first stage:

From:
RFP Analysis

To:
Opportunity Intelligence Assessment

The purpose of the first stage is:

"Understand the opportunity and determine what Risk Rising would need to deliver, support, implement and respond to."

DO NOT start with response generation.

FIRST PASS OUTPUT SHOULD IDENTIFY

1. Customer Objectives

What is the customer trying to achieve?

Examples:
- Modernise GRC
- Consolidate risk processes
- Improve regulatory compliance
- Establish TPRM
- Improve board reporting
- Create a connected risk model

2. Business Use Cases

Identify likely use cases.

Examples:
- Risk management
- Controls testing
- Incident management
- Vendor onboarding
- Third-party assessments
- Regulatory obligations
- Policy attestation
- Audit management
- Executive reporting

3. Capability Requirements

Identify required capabilities.

Map to:

- ERM
- Controls Compliance
- TPRM
- Regulatory Compliance
- Policy Management
- Incident Management
- Audit
- Assessments
- Reporting
- Integrations
- Workflow

4. LogicGate Mapping

If vendor = LogicGate:

Map capability requirements to LogicGate modules.

Examples:

Risk Register
→ LogicGate ERM

Vendor Assessments
→ LogicGate TPRM

Policy Attestation
→ LogicGate Policy Management

Incident Tracking
→ LogicGate Incident Management

Do not produce long capability descriptions.

Keep mappings concise.

5. Scope Assessment

Identify:

- in-scope capabilities
- likely out-of-scope capabilities
- mandatory requirements
- optional requirements

6. Suggested Delivery Phasing

Infer logical delivery phases.

Example:

Phase 1
- Foundation
- Taxonomy
- User model
- ERM
- Controls

Phase 2
- TPRM
- Regulatory
- Incidents

Phase 3
- Reporting
- Advanced workflows
- Optimisation

This is an RR recommendation, not a customer statement.

7. Resource Assessment

Estimate likely delivery roles required.

Examples:

- Project Manager
- Solution Architect
- LogicGate Consultant
- Business Analyst
- Trainer
- Data Migration Support
- Integration Specialist

8. Integration Assessment

Identify:

- likely integrations
- likely data sources
- API dependencies
- CCM dependencies

9. Data Migration Assessment

Identify:

- likely migration requirements
- complexity
- assumptions

10. Support Assessment

Identify:

- support expectations
- hypercare requirements
- managed service opportunities
- training obligations
- support windows

Examples:

Business hours support
24x7 requirement
Multi-region support
Follow-the-sun support

11. Geographic & Timezone Assessment

Identify:

- operating regions
- implementation timezone impacts
- support timezone impacts

Examples:

UK only
EMEA
Global
US coverage required
APAC coverage required

12. Risk Assessment

Identify:

- delivery risks
- integration risks
- resource risks
- platform risks
- dependency risks

13. Questions RR Needs Answered

Create a list of:

- customer clarification questions
- vendor clarification questions
- scope clarification questions

These are far more valuable than immediate draft responses.

14. Response Candidate Identification

Only after all analysis is complete:

Identify:

- requirements RR should respond to
- requirements LogicGate should validate
- requirements requiring joint response
- requirements that can be ignored

UI CHANGES

Replace current narrative-heavy output.

Create sections:

Opportunity Summary
Customer Objectives
Business Use Cases
Capability Mapping
LogicGate Mapping
Scope Assessment
Suggested Delivery Phases
Resource Assessment
Integration Assessment
Support Assessment
Timezone Assessment
Risks & Dependencies
Open Questions
Response Candidates

Remove:

- pursuit recommendation
- win strategy
- speculative managed service commentary
- marketing language
- long capability descriptions

DO NOT attempt to sell.

DO NOT attempt to write a proposal.

DO NOT attempt to recommend pursuit strategy.

Focus on understanding the opportunity.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

After running the assessment, Dev should be able to answer:

- What is the customer trying to achieve?
- What LogicGate modules are likely required?
- What RR services are required?
- What implementation approach makes sense?
- What phases make sense?
- What resources are needed?
- What support obligations exist?
- What timezone obligations exist?
- What are the biggest risks?
- What questions still need answering?
- Which requirements actually require a response?

Only after this stage is complete should the workflow move into:

Generate RR Responses
Generate Vendor Validation Pack
Generate Proposal
Generate Executive Deck

The Opportunity Intelligence Assessment becomes the foundation for every subsequent activity.
