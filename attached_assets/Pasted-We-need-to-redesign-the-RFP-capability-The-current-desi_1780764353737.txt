We need to redesign the RFP capability.

The current design is too focused on requirements, ownership classification and response generation.

This is not how Risk Rising reviews an RFP.

## New Principle

The purpose of the RFP tool is:

> To understand what Risk Rising would need to deliver, implement, support and respond to if we won the engagement.

The tool should behave like an experienced RR consultant reviewing an RFP for the first time.

The tool is NOT:

- a bid writer
- a proposal writer
- a requirement classifier
- a vendor capability explainer

The tool IS:

- a delivery assessor
- a support assessor
- a use case assessor
- a response prioritisation engine

---

# New Workflow

## Stage 1 – RFP Understanding

Analyse all uploaded documents.

This includes:

- RFPs
- RFIs
- Requirement spreadsheets
- Security questionnaires
- Procurement documents
- PDF decks
- Scope documents
- Supporting documents

Build an understanding of:

- what the customer is trying to achieve
- what capabilities they need
- what business problems they are solving

Do not generate responses yet.

---

## Stage 2 – Use Case Assessment

Identify:

### Primary Use Cases

Examples:

- Enterprise Risk Management
- Controls Management
- Internal Audit
- Third Party Risk Management
- Regulatory Compliance
- Policy Management
- Incident Management
- Risk Assessments
- Workflow Management
- Reporting

For each use case provide:

- confidence
- source references
- business importance

---

## Stage 3 – LogicGate Assessment

If vendorContext = LogicGate

Map use cases to LogicGate modules.

Example:

ERM
→ LogicGate ERM

Controls
→ Controls Compliance

TPRM
→ LogicGate TPRM

Policy
→ Policy Management

Incident Management
→ Incident Management

Do not generate marketing descriptions.

Keep mapping implementation-focused.

---

## Stage 4 – RR Delivery Assessment

This is the most important stage.

Determine:

### Discovery Activities

Would RR need:

- workshops
- requirements gathering
- process mapping
- operating model design

### Configuration Activities

Would RR need:

- workflow configuration
- forms
- reports
- dashboards
- notifications
- user roles

### Data Activities

Would RR need:

- data migration
- cleansing
- transformation
- taxonomy design

### Integration Activities

Would RR need:

- APIs
- ServiceNow
- HR systems
- ERP systems
- Security tools

### Testing Activities

Would RR need:

- UAT
- defect management
- go-live support

### Change Activities

Would RR need:

- training
- train-the-trainer
- adoption support

---

## Stage 5 – RR Support Assessment

Identify:

### Hypercare Requirements

### Training Requirements

### Administrative Support Requirements

### Managed Service Opportunities

### Platform Support Expectations

### Reporting Support Requirements

### Enhancement Requirements

Focus only on support implications for RR.

---

## Stage 6 – Geographic & Coverage Assessment

Identify:

### Regions

### Countries

### Languages

### Timezones

### Support Coverage Requirements

### Delivery Constraints

Only include information that impacts RR delivery or support.

---

## Stage 7 – Delivery Risks

Identify:

### Delivery Risks

### Integration Risks

### Data Risks

### Resource Risks

### Support Risks

### Timeline Risks

Only include risks relevant to RR delivery.

---

## Stage 8 – RR Response Identification

Only after all previous stages are complete.

Identify:

### Questions RR Must Answer

Examples:

- implementation approach
- migration strategy
- training approach
- support model
- managed service
- delivery governance
- resource model

### Questions LogicGate Must Validate

Examples:

- platform functionality
- security architecture
- hosting
- performance
- roadmap

### Questions Requiring Joint Response

Examples:

- integrations
- TPRM workflows
- reporting
- operating model

- sales strategy

Create four tabs only:

What business capabilities are required?

## 2. Delivery Impact

What RR would need to implement?

## 3. Support Impact

What RR would need to support?

## 4. Response Workbench

What questions actually require responses?

---

# Success Criteria

After analysing an RFP, Dev should immediately understand:

- what use cases exist
- what LogicGate modules are likely required
- what RR would need to deliver
- what RR would need to support
- what integrations are likely required
- what migration effort exists
- what resources are needed
- what timezones matter
- what delivery risks exist
- what questions RR actually needs to answer

If the output does not help estimate delivery scope, support scope or response effort, then the analysis has failed.
