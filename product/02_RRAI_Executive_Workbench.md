## 1. Purpose and Authority

### 1.1 Purpose

This document defines the **Executive Workbench**: the governed user environment through which leaders commission, develop, review and approve defined, high-value strategic, commercial, governance and delivery work. It answers one question: *what is the environment in which a bounded piece of high-value leadership work is completed to an exceptional standard, and what does that environment hold to?*

It gives the environment a single, settled shape — a unit of work (§5), a lifecycle (§6), a conceptual structure (§7), and firm boundaries (§12) — so that every part built for leaders to work in serves the same purpose and cannot drift into a different kind of product.

### 1.2 The core product position it expresses

This document does not restate the Product Philosophy or the Product Principles; it gives them a place to live. The position the Workbench exists to serve is:

> **RRAI helps leaders complete defined, high-value strategic, commercial, governance and delivery work to an exceptional standard**, combining structured reasoning, governed knowledge, evidence-led intelligence, reusable learning, and human judgement and approval — and **it does not organise a leader's general working day or run organisational operations.**

The Workbench is where that work is done. It is the place where a bounded piece of work is started; where its purpose, audience, outcome and deliverable are established; where evidence and governed knowledge are assembled; where reasoning is structured; where the deliverable is developed and refined; where human review and approval occur; and where reusable knowledge from completed work is retained through governed paths.

### 1.3 What this document is not

It is deliberately none of the following, and must not drift into any of them:

- **Not a redefinition of the Product Philosophy, Product Principles, Master Context or Platform Architecture.** It references them and depends on them; it restates none of them and may not alter, extend or soften any of them.
- **Not a new "Executive Work Model" document.** The Workbench is the user environment; it introduces no separate governing model of executive work, and this document creates no such document.
- **Not a deliverables catalogue.** It names no governed catalogue of deliverables and invents no catalogue filename. The catalogue of deliverables is owned elsewhere; this document only provides the environment in which a deliverable is developed.
- **Not an implementation.** It defines no database schema, API, state machine, navigation, screen layout or visual design. Those belong to the architecture, engineering and implementation areas.

## 4. Definition of the Executive Workbench

The **Executive Workbench** is the single governed user environment in which a leader completes a defined, high-value piece of work to an exceptional standard. It is where a bounded piece of work is scoped, evidenced, reasoned, developed, reviewed, approved, and turned into reusable knowledge — from the moment the work is started to the moment it is complete and its learning retained.

The Workbench is defined by four things:

- **It is work-centred.** The Workbench is organised around a bounded unit of work — the Work Item (§5) — and its deliverable, not around a person's day, an inbox, a calendar, or a stream of activity.
- **It is governed.** Everything done in the Workbench runs inside one active lens, meets the evidence and reasoning standard, and passes through human review and approval where consequence requires it (§8–§11).
- **It is an environment, not a feature.** The Workbench is the place work happens; conversation, evidence, reasoning, drafting, review and approval are areas within it (§7), not separate products.
- **It is bounded.** The Workbench does the defined work and stops. It does not expand to organise everything around the work (§12).

The Workbench is **not** a personal dashboard; a meeting or calendar organiser; a CRM; a project or task-management system; a generic chat interface; a system for running the organisation; a general monitoring centre; or a replacement for specialist operational systems (§12).

## 5. The Unit of Work

The Workbench is organised around a bounded unit of work. This document names that unit the **Work Item** and uses it as an **operational object inside the Workbench** — a way to bound and govern a single piece of work. It is not a new constitutional concept, not a new governing model, and not a deliverables catalogue; it introduces no authority above this document.

A Work Item is a single, bounded piece of defined, high-value work with an intended deliverable. At minimum, a Work Item carries:

- **Purpose** — the defined, high-value work it exists to complete, and why it matters.
- **Owner** — the named human accountable for the work and its outcome.
- **Audience** — who the deliverable is for and the standard they require.
- **Active lens** — the one governed lens the work runs in: Analyst, Intelligence, Commercial or Delivery (§11).
- **Intended deliverable** — the finished, human-owned output the work is to produce.
- **Scope** — what is included and, as importantly, what is not.
- **Evidence requirements** — the knowledge and evidence the work depends on, and the standard it must meet (§8).
- **Governing references** — the governed documents and knowledge the work must conform to.
- **Status** — where the work sits in the lifecycle (§6).
- **Approval requirements** — the human review and approval the deliverable must clear before it carries weight (§10).
- **Completion condition** — what must be true for the work to be considered done.

A Work Item exists only for defined work with a deliverable. Work that cannot state a purpose, an owner, a lens and an intended deliverable is not a Work Item and does not belong in the Workbench. The Work Item is the Workbench's answer to the Product Principles' Defined Work First and Deliverable First tests (Principles 4.1–4.2): it makes the defined work and its deliverable explicit before anything is produced.

## 6. Workbench Lifecycle

A Work Item moves through a simple, product-level lifecycle. The lifecycle describes the **shape of the work**, not its mechanics: it defines no state machine, no database status field, no API transition and no screen flow. Those are implementation matters owned elsewhere.

| Stage | What happens | What must hold |
|---|---|---|
| **Define** | The Work Item is established: purpose, owner, audience, active lens, intended deliverable, scope and completion condition are set. | The defined work and its deliverable are named before anything is produced (Principles 4.1–4.2). |
| **Gather** | The evidence and governed knowledge the work needs are assembled through the enforcement membrane. | Evidence is retrieved only as the active lens permits, and meets the standard in §8. |
| **Reason** | The problem is structured and the evidence is weighed; fact, interpretation and recommendation are kept apart. | Reasoning precedes generation (Principle 4.4); conflicts are surfaced, not resolved silently. |
| **Develop** | The deliverable is drafted from the structured reasoning. | The draft reflects the reasoning and carries its evidence and confidence (§9). |
| **Review** | The deliverable is tested against the standard it must meet and refined. | Quality is assessed against the audience and purpose; gaps are named. |
| **Approve** | A human reviews and approves the deliverable to the level its exposure requires. | The mandatory approval classes are honoured where they apply (§10; Platform Architecture §14). |
| **Complete** | The approved deliverable is finished and handed to its owner and audience. | The completion condition is met; the human owns the result. |
| **Reuse** | Reusable knowledge from the completed work is retained through governed paths. | Retention uses the governed Memory and Learning paths; nothing is promoted to authoritative Knowledge without human review (§8; Platform Architecture §15). |

The lifecycle is a description, not a rigid sequence: work may return to an earlier stage — for example, from Reason back to Gather when evidence is missing — but the two human gates are firm. Nothing is Approved without a human, and nothing reaches Complete without meeting its completion condition. The lifecycle remains product-level and implementation-independent; how each stage is realised in the runtime is a matter for the architecture and the engineering area.

## 7. Workbench Structure

The Workbench holds a small set of **conceptual areas** that together support a Work Item through its lifecycle. These are areas of the work, not screens: this document specifies no layout, navigation, component or visual design. The areas are:

- **Work Brief** — where the Work Item is defined and held: purpose, owner, audience, active lens, intended deliverable, scope, governing references, approval requirements and completion condition (§5).
- **Evidence** — where the governed knowledge and evidence the work depends on are assembled, through the enforcement membrane and under the standard in §8.
- **Reasoning** — where the problem is structured and the evidence weighed, keeping fact, interpretation and recommendation apart (§9).
- **Draft Deliverable** — where the deliverable is developed and refined from the reasoning (§9).
- **Review** — where the deliverable is tested against its standard and its gaps named (§10).
- **Approval** — where the human decision to accept and release the deliverable is made and recorded (§10).
- **Sources and References** — where the evidence, its tiers and confidence, and the governing references the work relied on are traceable.
- **Reusable Knowledge** — where knowledge from the completed work is captured for reuse through governed paths (§8; Platform Architecture §15).

These areas are conceptual and serve the Work Item; they are not standalone features and do not exist independently of defined work. How they are arranged, presented or navigated is an implementation and design matter outside this document.

### 7.1 Conversation

Conversation may be one way a leader interacts with the Workbench — a natural way to define work, ask for evidence, direct reasoning or request a change. But conversation is **an interface, not the product, and not the organising model.** The Workbench is organised around the Work Item and its deliverable, not around a chat thread.

Four things hold:

- **Chat is an interface, not the organising model.** The Workbench is structured by Work Items and their lifecycle (§5, §6); conversation is one means of working within that structure, never the structure itself.
- **The Work Item and its deliverable persist independently of any conversation.** They exist, advance and are owned whether or not a given conversation is open.
- **Conversations support the work but do not become the authoritative record.** What a leader and RRAI discuss informs the work; it is not the work's system of record.
- **Governed artefacts and approved outputs are authoritative — not chat history.** The Work Brief, the evidence and its sources, the reasoning, the approved deliverable and the retained reusable knowledge are what count; a transcript is not.

A design that made the chat thread the authoritative object, or that reduced the Workbench to a chat interface, would violate this document and the Product Principles (a generic chat interface is an excluded category, §12).

## 8. Evidence and Knowledge

The Workbench assembles evidence and governed knowledge for a Work Item **through the platform's enforcement membrane**, and under the evidence standard the platform already defines. This document adds no store, no retrieval mechanism and no evidence rule of its own; it inherits them.

Within a Work Item: evidence is retrieved only as the active lens permits, filtered for partition and sensitivity, and never read raw (Platform Architecture §9–§12); the evidence precedence T1–T5 applies, from approved governed Knowledge (T1) to model inference (T5) (Platform Architecture §12); conflicting evidence is surfaced, not resolved silently; and unverified material is used only under the conditions the architecture imposes, never as the sole basis for a consequential claim. Governed Knowledge is authoritative and is never altered from within a Work Item; the Workbench consumes it and does not write to approved Knowledge except through the governed human review gate (Platform Architecture §6, §15).

At the Reuse stage, knowledge from completed work is retained through the governed Memory and Learning paths — classified, marked by verification state, and proposed (never automatically promoted) to governed Knowledge through human review (Platform Architecture §7, §15). The Workbench is a place where reusable knowledge is *captured through governed paths*, not a new store and not a new write path.

## 9. Reasoning and Deliverable Development

The Workbench structures reasoning before it develops a deliverable. **Reasoning precedes generation** (Principle 4.4): a Work Item establishes the problem and the evidence, and structures how the problem is thought through, before any deliverable is drafted. A deliverable generated directly, without evidenced reasoning behind it, does not meet the Workbench's standard however polished it appears.

In the Reason and Develop stages, the Workbench keeps **fact, interpretation and recommendation apart**, carries the evidence and its confidence into the draft, and names gaps rather than filling them (Philosophy §2.4; Principles 4.3, 4.10). The deliverable is refined against its audience and purpose in the Review stage. Throughout, confidence and verification state are preserved and never silently dropped, and the active lens, evidence tiers relied upon, and any unverified-state flag travel with the work as the architecture requires (Platform Architecture §13). This document defines the *place and expectation* for reasoning and development; it defines no method, prompt, template or generation mechanism.

## 10. Human Review and Approval

RRAI recommends and prepares; a human decides, approves, commits and owns (Master Context §5; Philosophy §5; Principle 4.5). The Workbench makes that operative through its Review and Approve stages and adds no gate of its own beyond what the platform defines.

A deliverable is reviewed against the standard its audience and purpose require, and is **approved by a human to the level its exposure demands.** Where a Work Item's deliverable would involve any of the eight mandatory approval classes — external publication; customer commitments; pricing or commercial terms; legal or regulatory assertions; changes to approved Knowledge; sending communications; changes to opportunity or project status; or external system actions (Platform Architecture §14) — human approval is mandatory and non-negotiable. Internal work may be produced without approval but is labelled with its active lens, evidence tier and any unverified-state flag. No approval is inferred, and **no AI recommendation — from any model or agent — is ever treated as approval.** Approvals and consequential actions are recorded in the platform's audit trail.

## 11. Lens Behaviour

Every Work Item runs in **one active governed lens.** The lenses are the four established by the Master Context (§4) and enforced at runtime (Platform Architecture §9–§13): **Analyst**, **Intelligence**, **Commercial** and **Delivery**. This document does not redefine them and does not vary their number or nature.

Within the Workbench:

- **One lens per Work Item pass.** A Work Item declares its active lens, and its reasoning runs in that lens, inheriting the lens's permitted partitions, sensitivity ceiling, posture and approval rules (Platform Architecture §10, §13).
- **Cross-lens work runs as separate, lens-bound passes.** Where a piece of work genuinely needs more than one lens, it is done as separate lens-bound passes within the same Work Item where appropriate, never as a single pass spanning lenses. Neutral analysis may flow forward into Commercial or Delivery work; commercial or delivery context never flows back into Analyst outputs (the one-way valve, Master Context §6).
- **The active lens is always visible** and recorded, as the architecture requires.

There is **no Development lens.** Development, administration and control of the platform are an **administrator workspace or control plane**, not a fifth runtime lens. Any environment used to configure, govern or operate the platform sits outside the four-lens runtime model and must never be presented or treated as a lens through which leadership work is done. Introducing a new lens is a Master Context change, not a Workbench decision, and is escalated to the Product Owner (Principle 4.9; §15).

## 12. Workbench Boundaries

The Workbench does the defined work and stops. It must **not**, and must never drift to:

- organise a leader's general working day;
- manage calendars or meetings;
- manage generic tasks or projects;
- become a CRM;
- monitor an organisation continuously without a defined Work Item;
- replace operational systems;
- create work for itself without human direction.

Two rules hold the boundary. First, **everything in the Workbench attaches to a Work Item**: any capability, monitoring or automation exists only in service of a defined Work Item and its deliverable, and ends when that work ends — it does not become a standing surface. Second, **reference, do not replace** (Principle 4.13): where the work genuinely needs a calendar, a CRM, a project system, a meeting record or an operational system, the Workbench draws on it as a source or informs work carried out in it, and does not reproduce its function.

### 12.1 AI behaviour — anti-drift

AI participants working on the Workbench (per Development Operating Model §4 and Product Principles §10) must not drift the environment toward:

- morning dashboards;
- personal productivity;
- meeting management;
- organisational task orchestration;
- general business administration;
- generic development control centres.

The rule is simple: **the Workbench exists for completing defined, high-value work — not for organising everything around that work.** When a request moves the environment toward any of the excluded categories, an AI participant names the drift and stops, rather than designing it in; genuine ambiguity is escalated to the Product Owner (§15), never resolved by elaboration.

## 14. Conformance

Conformance is the standard everything built as part of the Workbench must meet. It is stated once here so that no lower artefact restates it.

**The Workbench product test.** Every part of the Workbench — every area, capability, journey or automation — must satisfy all of the following:

- it supports a defined Work Item;
- it improves a defined deliverable;
- it strengthens reasoning, evidence, quality or completion;
- it operates inside one governed lens;
- it preserves human judgement and approval;
- it does not reproduce generic productivity or operational tooling.

A part that fails any of these is non-conformant and is revised, rejected or escalated under the Product Principles admission test (Principles §9), of which this test is the Workbench-specific expression. Beyond admission, every part must continue to satisfy the test in operation, not only when it is built: a part that drifts out of conformance after release is a defect to be corrected or a change to be proposed (Development Operating Model §12). The Workbench must never exceed what the Product Philosophy and Product Principles permit; where it and a higher document diverge, the higher document prevails until amended.

This document is versioned. It is approved as **Version 1.0** by the Product Owner. Each subsequent approved amendment produces a new version, and the prior version is retired to read-only history. The current version is always identifiable, and the reasoning that produced it always recoverable.
