## Risk Rising's Target Markets

Risk Rising works primarily with organisations in regulated industries where GRC complexity is high, regulatory expectations are demanding, and the cost of poor risk management is significant. Core sectors include financial services, insurance, professional services, and technology-dependent enterprises.

---

## Financial Services

### GRC Profile

Financial services organisations face the most demanding and prescriptive regulatory environment of any sector. Key regulatory drivers:

- **FCA supervision** — Conduct requirements, Consumer Duty, SMCR personal accountability.
- **PRA prudential requirements** — Capital adequacy, operational resilience, liquidity.
- **DORA (EU-facing firms)** — Digital operational resilience and third-party ICT risk.
- **Basel III/IV** — Capital framework for banks.
- **SMCR** — Named accountability for senior managers.

### GRC Priorities

- Enterprise risk management integrated with regulatory capital frameworks.
- Conduct and compliance programmes meeting Consumer Duty requirements.
- Operational resilience — important business services, impact tolerances.
- TPRM programmes meeting FCA/PRA outsourcing and third-party risk requirements.
- Financial crime controls — AML, KYC, sanctions screening.
- Model risk management.

### LogicGate Use Cases in Financial Services

- Enterprise risk register with financial services risk taxonomy.
- Operational resilience management.
- Third-party risk management meeting FCA/PRA expectations.
- RCSA and KRI programmes.
- Audit management linked to risk.

## Insurance

### GRC Profile

Insurers are regulated by the PRA (UK) and subject to Solvency II / UK Solvency rules. Risk management is fundamental to the business model — insurers exist to manage and price risk.

### GRC Priorities

- Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA) — integrated risk and capital assessment.
- Operational risk management supporting Solvency II standard formula or internal model.
- Third-party and outsourcing risk management.
- Conduct and claims handling compliance.
- Cyber risk — growing exposure from underwriting and own operations.

## Technology and SaaS

### GRC Profile

Technology companies increasingly face security certification requirements (ISO 27001, SOC 2, Cyber Essentials) as a condition of enterprise customer contracts. Regulatory exposure grows with scale — GDPR, UK GDPR, and sector-specific requirements apply where they serve regulated industries.

### GRC Priorities

- ISO 27001 and SOC 2 certification programme management.
- Vendor risk management — supply chain security.
- GDPR/UK GDPR compliance and data protection programme.
- AI governance and EU AI Act readiness for AI-enabled products.
- Vulnerability management and patch tracking.

### LogicGate Use Cases in Technology

- ISO 27001 ISMS management and audit management.
- TPRM programme for SaaS supply chain.
- Policy management and attestation.
- Compliance obligation management.

## Professional Services

### GRC Profile

Consulting, accounting, legal, and advisory firms hold sensitive client data and are subject to professional standards, data protection, and client confidentiality obligations. Risk Rising itself operates in this sector.

### GRC Priorities

- Data protection and client data security.
- Professional indemnity and conflicts management.
- Quality management and engagement risk.
- Third-party and subcontractor management.

---

## Healthcare and Life Sciences

### GRC Profile

Healthcare organisations handle highly sensitive personal data and are subject to sector-specific regulatory frameworks. UK NHS organisations must comply with the Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT).

### GRC Priorities

- Information governance and NHS DSPT compliance.
- Data protection and patient confidentiality.
- Operational resilience and clinical risk.
- Third-party risk — particularly for medical devices and health IT suppliers.
- Regulatory submission and clinical trial compliance (life sciences).

---

## Energy and Utilities

### GRC Profile

Energy and utility companies are classified as critical infrastructure. They face growing regulatory requirements under NIS2 (EU), the UK Networks and Information Systems Regulations, and sector-specific OFGEM/OFWAT requirements.

### GRC Priorities

- Operational resilience and critical asset protection.
- Cyber security for operational technology (OT) and industrial control systems (ICS).
- Supply chain risk management.
- Environmental and ESG risk management.

---

## Public Sector

### GRC Profile

Public sector organisations are subject to UK government security requirements (Cyber Essentials), data protection (UK GDPR), and sector-specific standards. Many are subject to Freedom of Information Act obligations.

### GRC Priorities

- UK Government Security Policy Framework compliance.
- Data protection and UK GDPR.
- Procurement and supply chain risk management.
- IT and cyber security to Cyber Essentials standard.

## Common Cross-Sector GRC Patterns

Regardless of industry, organisations pursuing GRC programmes frequently encounter the same patterns:

1. **Risk fragmentation** — Multiple teams running parallel risk processes with no common taxonomy or reporting.
2. **Spreadsheet dependency** — Risk registers, control testing, and policy tracking managed manually.
3. **Compliance-only mindset** — GRC viewed as a tick-box exercise rather than a risk management discipline.
4. **Technology mismatch** — Legacy GRC tools that are difficult to configure, update, or integrate.
5. **Reporting gap** — Board and committee reporting that does not reflect operational risk reality.
