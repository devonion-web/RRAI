## EU Regulatory Landscape

### DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act

**Effective:** 17 January 2025
**Scope:** EU financial entities: banks, insurers, investment firms, payment institutions, and their critical ICT third-party providers.
**Key requirements:**
- ICT risk management framework with board accountability.
- ICT incident classification and mandatory reporting.
- Digital operational resilience testing (TLPT — threat-led penetration testing for significant entities).
- Third-party ICT risk management: register of ICT providers, contractual requirements, concentration risk monitoring.
- Oversight of Critical Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) by EU supervisory authorities.

**Implications for GRC:** DORA makes operational resilience and third-party risk management regulatory requirements, not just good practice. Organisations need a documented, tested ICT risk management framework and a complete third-party ICT register.

### NIS2 Directive — Network and Information Security

**Effective:** October 2024 (EU member state transposition)
**Scope:** Significantly broader than NIS1 — covers essential and important entities across 18 sectors including energy, transport, financial services, health, digital infrastructure, and ICT service management.
**Key requirements:**
- Risk management measures for network and information systems.
- Incident reporting obligations (24-hour initial warning; 72-hour detailed report; final report within one month).
- Supply chain security — managing risks from third parties.
- Senior management accountability for cybersecurity compliance.
- Substantial fines for non-compliance (up to €10M or 2% of global turnover for essential entities).

### EU AI Act

**Effective:** Phased from 2024–2027
**Scope:** All AI systems used or placed on the market in the EU.
**Key requirements:**
- Risk-based classification: prohibited practices (unacceptable risk), high-risk AI systems, limited risk, minimal risk.
- High-risk AI systems require conformity assessment, transparency, human oversight, and robustness requirements.
- General Purpose AI (GPAI) models have additional transparency and risk management obligations.

### EU GDPR

**Scope:** Processing of personal data of EU individuals. UK GDPR (retained law) applies in the UK.
**Key requirements:** Lawful basis for processing; data subject rights; privacy notices; DPIAs for high-risk processing; 72-hour breach notification to supervisory authority; data transfer restrictions.

## UK Regulatory Landscape

### UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018

UK equivalent of EU GDPR, enforced by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). Largely mirrors EU GDPR. The ICO can issue fines up to £17.5M or 4% of annual global turnover, whichever is higher.

### FCA Consumer Duty

**Effective:** 31 July 2023 (new and existing products); 31 July 2024 (closed book products)
**Scope:** FCA-regulated firms with retail customers.
**Key requirement:** Firms must deliver good outcomes for retail customers across four outcome areas: Products and Services, Price and Value, Consumer Understanding, Consumer Support. Board-approved self-assessment required.

### FCA Operational Resilience Policy

**Effective:** March 2022 (self-assessment); March 2025 (full compliance)
**Key requirements:** Identify important business services; set impact tolerances; ensure operations remain within tolerances during severe but plausible scenarios; maintain continuity plans.

### UK Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus

Government-backed scheme for baseline cyber security. Required for UK central government contract suppliers handling personal or commercially sensitive data.

## Global and International Standards

### ISO/IEC 27001:2022

International standard for information security management systems (ISMS). Certifiable. Requires risk-based approach to information security. Latest version (2022) reorganises Annex A controls into four themes: Organisational, People, Physical, Technological.

### SOC 2 — System and Organisation Controls

AICPA framework for service organisations. Type I (design effectiveness) and Type II (operating effectiveness over a period). Trust Service Criteria: Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy. Widely required in B2B sales, particularly SaaS providers.

### PCI DSS — Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard

Requirements for organisations that store, process, or transmit cardholder data. Administered by the PCI Security Standards Council. PCI DSS v4.0 published 2022; full compliance required by March 2025.

### ISO 22301 — Business Continuity Management

International standard for BCM systems. Certifiable. Requires risk assessment, BIA, BCP development, testing, and continual improvement.

## Sector-Specific Regulations

### Financial Services

- **Basel III/IV** — Prudential capital and liquidity requirements for banks.
- **Solvency II** — Prudential framework for EU/UK insurers.
- **MiFID II** — Markets in Financial Instruments Directive — conduct of business and market structure.
- **EMIR** — European Market Infrastructure Regulation — OTC derivatives.
- **SMCR** — Senior Managers and Certification Regime (UK) — personal accountability for financial services leaders.

### Healthcare

- **NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT)** — UK NHS information security and data protection requirements.
- **HIPAA** — US healthcare data privacy and security (relevant for US-market engagements).
- **MDR/IVDR** — EU Medical Device and In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation.

### Technology and Critical Infrastructure

- **CRA — Cyber Resilience Act (EU)** — Security requirements for products with digital elements (2024–2027 phased implementation).
- **CRITICAL Entities Resilience Directive (CER)** — Physical resilience of critical infrastructure.

## Regulatory Horizon Monitoring

Organisations must actively monitor the regulatory landscape rather than reacting after effective dates:

- Subscribe to regulatory update services (FCA, ICO, Bank of England).
- Monitor EU Official Journal for new regulations and directives.
- Engage specialist legal and regulatory advisers.
- Build horizon scanning into the compliance function's annual programme.

Regulatory change is a source of compliance risk and should appear on the risk register where it is material.
