## What is Compliance Management?

Compliance management is the process by which an organisation ensures it meets applicable laws, regulations, standards, contracts, and internal policies. In regulated industries, compliance is not optional — failure can result in regulatory sanction, fines, reputational damage, and loss of operating licence.

Effective compliance management is risk-based: not all obligations carry equal risk, and resources should be allocated proportionate to the risk of non-compliance and the consequences of a breach.

## The Compliance Lifecycle

### 1. Obligation Identification

The first step is building a complete inventory of applicable compliance obligations:

- **Legislation and regulation** — Acts of Parliament, statutory instruments, EU retained law, sector-specific regulations.
- **Regulatory rules** — FCA Handbook, PRA Rulebook, ICO guidance, sector regulator requirements.
- **Standards** — ISO, NIST, PCI DSS, and other voluntary or contractually required standards.
- **Contracts** — Customer, supplier, and partner agreements with compliance obligations.
- **Internal policies** — Board-approved policies that create internal compliance obligations.

Obligation libraries should be maintained and updated as the regulatory landscape changes.

### 2. Obligation Assessment

Each obligation is assessed to determine:

- Applicability (does it apply to this entity, product, or process?).
- The control or behaviour required.
- The function or individual accountable.
- The risk if the obligation is not met.

### 3. Control Mapping

Compliance obligations are mapped to the controls that give effect to them. One control may satisfy multiple obligations (control sharing). One obligation may require multiple controls. This mapping enables efficient compliance testing and avoids duplication across teams.

### 4. Control Testing

Controls are tested to verify:

- **Design effectiveness** — The control is appropriately designed to meet the obligation.
- **Operating effectiveness** — The control operates as designed in practice.

Testing may be performed by the compliance function, the first line (self-assessment), or by internal audit.

### 5. Reporting and Escalation

Non-compliance identified through testing or monitoring must be escalated appropriately:

- Breaches of regulatory rules may require regulatory notification.
- Material control failures are reported to senior management and the board.
- Remediation plans are agreed and tracked.

### 6. Regulatory Change Management

Regulations change. Organisations must monitor the regulatory horizon, assess the impact of changes, and update controls, policies, and procedures accordingly. Horizon scanning is a dedicated compliance activity.

## Compliance Risk Assessment

Compliance risk is assessed on two dimensions:

- **Likelihood of non-compliance** — Based on the complexity of the obligation, control maturity, and past performance.
- **Impact of non-compliance** — Financial (fines, litigation), operational (licence suspension), and reputational.

A compliance risk assessment matrix enables prioritisation of the compliance programme and audit plan.

## Regulatory Compliance in Financial Services

### FCA Compliance Obligations

FCA-regulated firms must comply with the FCA Handbook, which covers: Conduct of Business, Systems and Controls (SYSC), Client Assets (CASS), Market Conduct (MAR), and the Consumer Duty (effective July 2023). The Consumer Duty requires firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers across four outcome areas: Products and Services, Price and Value, Consumer Understanding, and Consumer Support.

### PRA Requirements

PRA-supervised firms (banks and insurers) have additional prudential obligations covering capital adequacy, liquidity, governance, and operational resilience.

### UK GDPR and Data Protection

The UK GDPR (retained from EU GDPR post-Brexit) regulates the processing of personal data. Key requirements: lawful basis for processing; data subject rights (access, erasure, portability); privacy notices; data protection impact assessments (DPIAs); breach notification within 72 hours.

## Compliance Programme Design

### Compliance Framework

A compliance framework sets out the organisation's approach to compliance management: governance, roles and responsibilities, processes, tools, and reporting lines. It should be approved by the board and reviewed regularly.

### Compliance Culture

Technology and process alone cannot deliver compliance. A compliance culture requires:

- Tone from the top — visible commitment from leadership.
- Clear accountability — staff understand their compliance obligations.
- No-blame reporting — staff can raise concerns without fear.
- Compliance as an enabler — not a barrier.

### Training and Awareness

Regular training is a compliance requirement in most regulated sectors. Training should be role-relevant, updated when regulations change, and completion tracked and attested.

## Common Compliance Challenges

- Obligation library incomplete or not maintained.
- Compliance obligations not mapped to controls — testing is ad hoc.
- Control testing performed by the first line only — no independent validation.
- Regulatory change not identified until after the effective date.
- Compliance activity siloed from risk management and internal audit.
- Over-reliance on manual processes — spreadsheets, email.
- Board and senior management not engaged with compliance status.
