## What is Internal Audit?

Internal audit is an independent assurance and advisory function that helps an organisation achieve its objectives by bringing a systematic, disciplined approach to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control processes. It is the third line of defence in the Three Lines Model.

Internal audit reports to the board (typically through the audit committee) to ensure independence from management. This independence is fundamental — it distinguishes internal audit from risk management and compliance (the second line).

---

## The Role of Internal Audit

### Assurance

Internal audit's primary function is to provide independent assurance that the organisation's risk management, governance, and internal control frameworks are operating effectively. This includes:

- Evaluating whether controls exist and are well-designed.
- Testing whether controls operate as designed in practice (operating effectiveness).
- Assessing whether risks are being managed within the board's approved appetite.
- Reviewing compliance with laws, regulations, and internal policies.

### Advisory

Beyond assurance, internal audit may provide advisory services to management, including:

- Input to new processes, systems, or controls during design (before implementation).
- Risk-based guidance on operational changes.
- Facilitation of control self-assessment.

Advisory engagements must not compromise independence — auditors should not audit activity they have directly shaped.

## The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA)

The IIA is the global professional body for internal auditors. Its core standards and guidance:

- **International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing (IPPF)** — the globally recognised standards framework.
- **Core Principles** — Ten principles that guide effective internal audit practice.
- **Code of Ethics** — Integrity, objectivity, confidentiality, and competency.
- **Quality Assurance and Improvement Programme (QAIP)** — Internal audit functions should maintain a continuous improvement programme and undergo external quality assessment every five years.

## Audit Lifecycle

### 1. Risk-Based Audit Planning

The annual audit plan is driven by the organisation's risk profile. Internal audit maps its resources to the highest-risk areas, taking into account:

- The risk register and risk appetite.
- Areas of significant change (new systems, reorganisations, acquisitions).
- Regulatory requirements and expectations.
- Prior audit findings not yet remediated.
- Management and audit committee concerns.

The audit plan should be reviewed and approved by the audit committee. It should be revisited during the year as the risk landscape evolves.

### 2. Engagement Planning

For each audit engagement:

- Define scope, objectives, and approach.
- Conduct a preliminary risk assessment for the area under review.
- Identify key controls.
- Prepare an audit programme (testing plan).
- Communicate scope and timeline to the business.

### 3. Fieldwork

Fieldwork is the evidence-gathering phase:

- Interview control owners and process participants.
- Observe processes in operation.
- Inspect documents and records.
- Test a sample of transactions against control requirements.
- Use data analytics to analyse populations and identify anomalies.

### 4. Reporting

Audit findings are communicated to management and the audit committee:

- **Draft report** — Shared with management for factual accuracy and response.
- **Final report** — Includes management's agreed actions and target completion dates.
- **Rating** — Findings are typically rated by severity: Critical, High, Medium, Low, or Informational.

### 5. Follow-Up

Outstanding actions must be tracked to completion. Internal audit verifies that agreed actions have been implemented. Overdue or unaddressed high-risk findings are escalated to the audit committee.

## Audit Types

### Operational Audits

Assess the efficiency and effectiveness of operational processes. Focus: are processes achieving their objectives with appropriate use of resources?

### Compliance Audits

Assess whether activities conform to applicable laws, regulations, standards, and policies.

### Financial Controls Audits

Review the controls over financial reporting, transaction processing, and accounting. Particularly important in regulated entities.

### IT / Technology Audits

Assess IT governance, cybersecurity controls, system access management, change management, data integrity, and application controls.

### Risk Management Audits

Assess the effectiveness of the risk management framework itself — not just individual controls. Are risks being identified, assessed, and managed appropriately?

### Forensic and Investigation Engagements

Respond to suspected fraud, misconduct, or regulatory breach. These are typically separate from the regular audit plan and require specialist skills.

## Key Audit Concepts

### Control Design vs Operating Effectiveness

A control can be well-designed but not operating effectively, or operating for some transactions but not others. Both dimensions must be assessed.

### Population vs Sample

Internal audit often tests a sample of transactions. Sample size should be risk-based; statistical sampling provides a more defensible basis but is not always required.

### Root Cause Analysis

Good audit findings go beyond describing what happened to identifying why. Root cause analysis (inadequate training, poor process design, system limitation, management override) helps management implement durable fixes.

### Management Action Plans

Each finding should have an agreed management action, a named owner, and a realistic target completion date. Vague actions ("improve the process") are not acceptable.

## Common Internal Audit Challenges

- Insufficient resources to cover the full audit universe.
- Independence compromised by management pressure.
- Audit findings not connected to the risk register.
- Action tracking managed in spreadsheets rather than a system.
- Repeat findings — management actions not implemented or not effective.
- Audit reports too long and too technical for the audit committee.
- Limited use of data analytics despite available data.
- Annual audit plan not updated to reflect emerging risks.
